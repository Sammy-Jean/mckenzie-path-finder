Why it's tempting

  • You're earning in about a year, not four.
  • Creative work, and you see results the same day.
  • Tips add roughly 10–25% on top of wages. Top stylists nationally make about $71k, and owning a salon can go higher.
  • Pell can be used at accredited cosmetology schools too, so ask each school.

What to watch

  • The bottom 10% make around $27k. Building a client list takes years.
  • Commission splits or booth rent come out of what you earn. Ask salons exactly how they pay.
  • Local pay runs below the national median ($35,790). The Youngstown–Warren median was lower than every big Ohio metro in Raphael's own data.
  • On your feet all day, nights and Saturdays are prime time.
  • Many schools cost more than one year of Pell covers.

A possible ladder

  1. Year 1School + state board exam
  2. Years 2–4Assistant or new stylist, building clients
  3. Years 4–7Established stylist, maybe a specialty (color, extensions, lashes)
  4. LaterBooth rental, educator, manager, or your own salon (which is where business skills come in)

Getting into college

Good news: Kent State Trumbull is open admission for anyone with a high school diploma or the equivalent, and no ACT or SAT is required. Kent also accepts a homeschool transcript your family makes, as long as it has the right pieces.

Your main route

A homeschool transcript your family makes

You don't need to be in an official homeschool program. Kent asks homeschooled applicants for two things:

  • A typed transcript listing your courses and grades for grades 9–12 and showing you graduated. It needs your homeschool's name and address, the date, and a handwritten signature from your parent.
  • A letter from your school district superintendent confirming your homeschool status. Ohio homeschoolers notify the district every year, so ask for your most recent acknowledgment letter. If there isn't one on file, get that sorted first.
  • Build it around Ohio's college-prep list: 4 years English, 4 years math (through Algebra II), 3 science, 3 social studies, 2 foreign language, and 1 art. Your ASL can cover the foreign language. Anything else missing is what to work on now.
  • Your GPA on it matters. It's what qualifies you for merit scholarships (see Cost & time). Keep the grades honest, because placement tests will show where you really are.
Makes your transcript stronger

College Credit Plus (CCP) while still homeschooling

Ohio lets homeschooled students in grades 7–12 take real college classes, and the state can cover the cost.

  • College grades on a Kent transcript back up your homemade transcript. They're proof you can do college work.
  • You stay a homeschooler, so you can still play high school softball.
  • Credits count toward your Kent degree and your high school transcript.
  • To qualify you generally need a 3.0 unweighted GPA or college-ready placement test scores.
  • Homeschoolers apply separately for state funding, and there's a letter of intent due by April 1 for the next school year.
Probably not your route

GED now

A GED would get you into Kent, but in your situation it costs more than it gives you:

  • Ohio requires a school withdrawal form to take the GED at 16, 17, or 18, plus a notarized parent consent form if you're under 18. That likely ends high school softball and CCP.
  • A GED has no GPA, so you could miss out on GPA-based scholarships.
  • One exception to know about: Raphael's cosmetology school only takes homeschoolers with a diploma from a state-approved program, a GED, or a diploma certified by your superintendent. If you choose cosmetology, call them and ask whether your transcript will work before planning around a GED.
Smart no matter what

Check where you are, without committing

The GED Ready practice tests on GED.com show whether you're at a high school level in math, reading, science, and social studies. Taking a practice test doesn't require withdrawing from anything. It's a cheap checkup that tells you which subjects to work on before college placement tests.

Your study plan

Getting into Trumbull is the easy part. Placement tests decide which classes you start in, and low scores mean non-credit catch-up classes that cost time and grant money. A few months of focused work now can skip all of that.

How placement works at Kent: every new student takes the ALEKS math placement, which is online and takes 60–90 minutes. If you don't have ACT or SAT scores, regional campuses also use Accuplacer for reading and writing. Kent covers the cost of ALEKS, a six-week learning module, and a retake, so you get a second shot.

Your ASL is a big deal 🤟

Knowing American Sign Language puts you ahead in more ways than you might think. Here's where it helps.

On your transcript

Ohio's college-prep list asks for 2 years of a foreign language. List your ASL there. Kent counts its ASL courses as foreign language, so it's a real credential, not just a hobby.

Possible free college credit

Kent invites students who learned ASL, in classes or informally, to take an ASL placement test by emailing MCLS@kent.edu. If you place above the first course, you may be able to earn credit for what you already know. Elementary ASL I and II are 4 credits each.

An edge in any job

Very few stylists, project coordinators, or IT techs can sign. You could serve Deaf clients other salons can't, handle accessibility in a business or HR role, or support Deaf users in IT. Put it on every resume and application.

A path of its own

Kent State offers a bachelor's in American Sign Language and one in ASL/English Interpreting. If you love signing, it's worth looking at alongside business and IT. Ask which campuses offer them.

Beyond the paycheck

A path isn't just a job. It's who you meet, what you try, and how big your world gets.

College (in person)

Classes in subjects you've never tried, which is how a lot of people find what they actually love. Clubs, rec sports, student government, internships, study abroad, and people from different backgrounds. Professors and advisors who can write recommendations and open doors. Plenty of people change majors once they see what's out there, and that's normal.

College (online, like the IT degree)

The same degree and the same doors afterward, with more flexibility. But fewer of the campus experiences happen on their own, so you'd have to go after them on purpose, for example by joining Trumbull clubs or rec sports, or taking some classes in person.

Cosmetology

New experiences come from the work itself: meeting new clients every day, hair shows, competitions, advanced classes, and maybe one day working at weddings, on photo shoots, or in another city. The world it opens up is real, just narrower and focused on one field.

Something to think about: which kind of new experiences sound exciting to you? Trying lots of different things to see what clicks, or going deep on one thing you already know you like?

It doesn't have to be one or the other

Some of the strongest plans mix paths. A few to think about:

Future salon owner

Get the cosmetology license first and work, then take business classes part-time. Owning a salon is a business management job.

Degree with a side hustle

Do the BBA, and keep hair or nails as a fun side skill. Some career-tech centers let high schoolers finish cosmetology hours before graduating, so ask if homeschoolers can join.

Business + tech

BBA major with IT or data classes as electives. "Business analyst" and "IT project coordinator" love people who can talk to both sides.

Head start with CCP

Stay homeschooled, play softball, and knock out college classes for free now. You'd show up at Kent with credits already done.

About the CAPM cert: it's the entry-level project management certification. You need a high school diploma or GED plus 23 hours of project management education. The exam is $225 for PMI members or $300 for non-members. It won't land a PM job by itself, but it shows you're serious. It pairs best with a degree or some real work experience.
